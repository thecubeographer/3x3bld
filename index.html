    Why a solve ends one piece wrong

    Letter a spot on the cube. Every sticker has one. A letter is a place, not a piece.
    Buffer the one spot you never aim at. For corners it is A. You read whatever sticker is sitting there.
    Shot one algorithm. It swaps the piece in the buffer with the piece at the letter you read. One letter, one shot.
    Chain read the buffer, shoot, read the buffer, shoot, until the buffer's own piece comes back home.
    Break-in the buffer piece is home but other pieces are still wrong. You shoot at any wrong piece to start a new chain. That chain ends by shooting back at that same piece.
    Twist a piece in the right spot but turned. It is a chain of its own: two shots on the same piece.

    A break-in costs one extra shot, at the end

    • Grey pieces are already fine. Only the coloured ones matter.
    • Buffer home, pieces still wrong: shoot any wrong piece. That starts a new chain.
    • Keep reading the buffer like normal.
    • The chain ends by shooting back at the piece you started it on. Underlined above.
    • Which letter of that piece? Whatever the buffer shows. Read it, do not guess.

    How to know if you need the extra shot

    • Count the wrong corners. Do not count the buffer.
    • Add one for every break-in. A twist counts as a break-in.
    • That total is how many letters you must have. If your memo is shorter, a break-in is missing its last shot.
    • Faster check: every chain you started with a break-in must end on the piece you started it on. If it does not, you are not done.

    A twist is two shots on one piece

    • The coloured corner is in the right spot but turned.
    • Shoot one of its letters. Then the buffer shows another letter of that same piece. Shoot that too.
    • Two letters, two algorithms, one corner. The Solution sheet labels it twist.

    Parity

    • Count your corner letters. Odd means do the parity algorithm. Even means skip it.
    • Do it once, after the last corner, before the first edge.
    • 2x2 never has parity.

    2x2: one corner never moves

    • This corner is your fixed point. You never shoot at it.
    • If a solve ends with it wrong, you turned the whole cube mid-solve.
    • Everything else works exactly like 3x3 corners.
